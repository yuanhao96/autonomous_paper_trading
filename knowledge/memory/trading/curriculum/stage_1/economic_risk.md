## 2026-03-08 — Financial risk management

Foreign exchange risk encompasses three main types—transaction, translation, and economic risk—that affect businesses and investors engaged in cross-currency activities. Effective management requires hedging strategies including forwards, options, and natural hedging, while currency diversification offers protection against inflation and government debt devaluation. The forex market's 24/5 operation across global sessions with $9.6 trillion daily volume provides liquidity and early warning signals for intermarket analysis.

**Key concepts:** Foreign exchange risk (FX risk), Transaction risk, Translation risk, Economic risk, Currency diversification, Hedging, Forward contracts, Options, Natural hedging, Intermarket analysis, Carry trade, Value at risk (VaR), Financial risk management, Trading sessions (Tokyo/London/New York), Liquidity risk

**Trading implications:**
- Maintain multi-currency exposure to hedge against government-induced inflation and debt devaluation
- Trade during London-New York session overlap for peak liquidity and volatility
- Use forex markets as early warning signals for other asset classes to improve risk-adjusted returns
- Hedge transaction exposure using forward contracts and options
- Implement natural hedging by netting foreign currency inflows and outflows
- Diversify export markets and production sites to reduce economic risk exposure

**Risk factors:**
- Exchange rate fluctuations affecting contractual cash flows (transaction risk)
- Unfavorable currency movements impacting reported earnings and stock price (translation risk)
- Macroeconomic conditions and geopolitical instability affecting market value (economic risk)
- Liquidity risk from unpredictable currency fluctuations
- Contingent risk from pending contracts or negotiations
- Government regulations and political stability changes in foreign jurisdictions
- Currency crises leading to substantial losses
