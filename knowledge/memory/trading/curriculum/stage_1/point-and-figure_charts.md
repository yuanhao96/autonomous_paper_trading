## 2026-03-11 — Technical Analysis - The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians 2nd edition 2010 — part 3

This collection of documents covers foundational technical analysis concepts including trend analysis, chart construction, and pattern recognition. Key topics include point-and-figure charting methodology, candlestick patterns, bar chart analysis, and various trend determination techniques. The materials emphasize price action as the primary driver of technical analysis, with specific attention to breakout confirmation, support/resistance levels, and reversal patterns across multiple chart types.

**Key concepts:** Trend analysis and Dow Theory, Point-and-figure charting (X's and O's, box sizes, reversal patterns), Candlestick charting (real body, shadows, engulfing patterns), Bar charts (high, low, close, volume), Support and resistance levels, Breakout confirmation and trading ranges, Relative strength analysis, Three-line break, Renko, and Kagi charts, Planetary cycle analysis (astro-trading), Cup with handle pattern, Arithmetic vs. semi-logarithmic scale

**Trading implications:**
- Buy signals in point-and-figure occur when an X column exceeds a previous X column; sell signals when an O column falls below a previous O column
- Wait for breakout confirmation on volume at least 50% above normal rather than anticipating breakouts
- Use three-point reversal charts for standard analysis and five-point charts for long-term trends
- Enter trades at pivot points after price consolidation patterns complete, not at absolute lows
- Confirm simple buy signals with improving relative strength before taking positions
- Place protective stops to manage risk on false breakouts
- Trailing stops should be adjusted as trends develop to protect profits
- Candlestick patterns provide earlier reversal warnings than bar charts (2-3 sessions vs. weeks)

**Risk factors:**
- False breakouts ('specialist breakouts') can trigger premature entries
- Chasing stocks more than 5-10% above buy points increases risk significantly
- One-point reversal charts may be too sensitive and generate excessive noise
- Simple buy signals without relative strength confirmation have higher failure rates
- Windows (gaps) in candlestick charts often become resistance areas on rebounds
- Planetary/astro-based trading lacks empirical validation and may introduce confirmation bias
