The documents synthesize fundamental valuation metrics (EPS, P/E ratios) with technical analysis methodologies from Dow Theory. Key focus areas include weighted average shares outstanding as a critical denominator in EPS calculations, the distinction between basic and diluted EPS, and the complementary use of fundamental valuation (trailing/forward P/E) with technical timing tools (trend analysis, breakouts, support/resistance).

**Key concepts:** Basic EPS, Diluted EPS, Weighted Average Shares Outstanding, Trailing P/E (TTM/LTM), Forward P/E, Dow Theory, Trend Analysis, Support and Resistance, Breakouts, PEG Ratio, Earnings Yield, Relative P/E, Convertible Securities, Treasury Stock, Dilution

**Trading implications:**
- Use trailing P/E for mature companies with stable earnings history
- Use forward P/E for growth assessment and future profitability evaluation
- Enter positions on confirmed breakouts with volume confirmation
- Place protective stops below support level
- Complement fundamental valuation with technical timing for entry/exit decisions

**Risk factors:**
- Dilution from convertible securities reduces proportional ownership
- Stock buybacks and issuances distort apparent share count if not time-weighted
- Forward P/E relies on analyst estimates which may be inaccurate
- Breakout signals require volume confirmation to avoid false signals
- Basic EPS understates potential dilution for companies with complex capital structures
