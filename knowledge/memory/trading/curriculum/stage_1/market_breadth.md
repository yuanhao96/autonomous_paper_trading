## 2026-03-11 — Technical Analysis - The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians 2nd edition 2010 — part 2

Market breadth indicators measure the number of advancing versus declining stocks to assess index health and trend participation. These technical tools, including the Advance-Decline Line, Arms Index (TRIN), and various breadth ratios, help traders identify divergences between price action and underlying market strength, providing early warning signals for potential reversals.

**Key concepts:** Market Breadth, Advance-Decline Line (ADL), Arms Index (TRIN), Breadth Thrust, Ninety Percent Downside Days, Net New Highs/Lows, Hindenburg Omen, High Low Logic Index, Double Negative Divergence, Confirmation vs Divergence, Positive/Negative Market Breadth, Volume-Weighted Breadth

**Trading implications:**
- Monitor ADL to 32-week SMA for trend confirmation signals
- Watch for double negative divergences as early reversal warnings
- Use 90% downside days to identify panic selling opportunities
- Track 10-to-1 up volume days for momentum confirmation
- Apply Arms Index (TRIN) to identify short-term overbought/oversold conditions
- Compare index price action with breadth indicators to spot hidden weakness

**Risk factors:**
- Breadth indicators may fail to predict price reversals or signal too early
- Index can rise despite negative breadth when few large-cap stocks drive gains
- Traditional advance-decline methods have become less profitable over time
- Volume-based breadth signals can generate false positives in low-liquidity environments
- Divergence signals require confirmation before acting
