These documents synthesize technical analysis methodologies from Dow Theory and trend-based trading with fundamental valuation metrics, particularly P/E ratios (trailing TTM/LTM vs. forward). Key themes include trend identification through peaks/troughs and support/resistance, market sentiment measurement, the complementary use of technical and fundamental analysis, and practical trading applications including stop placement and breakout confirmation. The materials also cover forex diversification needs and the limitations of analyst estimates for forward valuations.

**Key concepts:** Trend Analysis, Dow Theory, Support and Resistance, Trailing P/E (TTM/LTM), Forward P/E, Market Breadth, Advance-Decline Line, Sentiment Indicators, Breakouts, Stop Orders, Point-and-Figure Charts, Behavioral Finance, Market Efficiency Hypothesis, Forex Diversification, PEG Ratio, Confirmation (Dow Theory), Volume Analysis

**Trading implications:**
- Use trailing P/E for mature, stable companies with consistent earnings; forward P/E for high-growth or transitioning companies
- Confirm breakouts with volume and closing prices beyond thresholds; use stops to manage false breakout risk
- Apply Dow Theory's three trend classifications (primary, secondary, minor) for multi-timeframe analysis
- Measure market sentiment through options data, polls, and contrary opinion indicators to identify extremes
- Use advance-decline line divergences to spot weakening market internals before price peaks
- Implement trailing stops to protect profits and time stops to exit non-performing positions
- Diversify currency exposure to hedge against inflationary government debt policies
- Compare trailing vs. forward P/E gap to assess market growth expectations and estimate reliability

**Risk factors:**
- Analyst estimates for forward P/E are wrong 25% of the time by 20%+ and tend toward optimism
- Traditional advance-decline methods have become unprofitable due to market evolution
- False breakouts ('specialist breakouts') require stop placement beyond normal retracement zones
- Trailing P/E can be distorted by one-time items and stale data up to 3 months old
- Wide analyst estimate dispersion reduces forward P/E reliability
- Random walk and market efficiency hypotheses challenge technical analysis validity
- Fat tails and drawdowns exceed normal distribution assumptions
- Currency depreciation risk from inflationary government policies
- Crowd behavior and herding create sentiment extremes that reverse abruptly
